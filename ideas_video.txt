VIDEO IDEAS - Senus PLC Board Report
====================================

SUGGESTED LENGTH: 4-6 minutes, in English

1. HOOK (0:00-0:20)
   - The problem: financial information is locked inside long PDFs and is hard to review and compare.
   - The product: an end-to-end workflow from source document to reviewable financial dashboard.

2. PRODUCT OVERVIEW (0:20-0:50)
   - Three areas: Documents, Financials and Company.
   - Admins upload, review and publish; viewers explore published results.
   - Brief architecture: React + TypeScript, FastAPI, PostgreSQL and Docker Compose.

3. DOCUMENT WORKFLOW (0:50-2:00)
   - Upload a PDF, detect reporting periods and confirm dates before processing.
   - Extract P&L, balance sheet, cash flow and relevant narrative.
   - Deterministic parser plus optional AI-assisted extraction; compare results and flag disagreements.
   - Source page references, confidence factors, review/correction and publish flow.
   - Accounting checks expose discrepancies; missing data is not silently invented.

4. FINANCIALS (2:00-3:20)
   - KPIs and financial statements with reported, derived and unavailable values identified.
   - Formulas and source provenance help explain where figures come from.
   - Period comparisons always run chronologically; warn when durations differ.
   - Waterfall explains the change in operating result.

5. COMPANY AND AI (3:20-3:55)
   - Briefly show customers, milestones and management as narrative extracted from the report.
   - Insights and chat are AI-generated interpretations, not quotations or financial sources.
   - Senus is the sample report, not the main subject of the presentation.

6. ENGINEERING AND RELIABILITY (3:55-4:30)
   - Backend (pytest) and frontend (vitest) tests.
   - Deterministic calculations, validation, reconciliation, login and role-based access.
   - AI is optional and kept out of the financial calculation path.

7. WRAP-UP (4:30-4:50)
   - Design principle: preserve sources, show calculations, flag uncertainty, leave gaps visible.
   - Call to action: repository / README.
   - One-line summary: "From the PDF to a report you can inspect and use."

RECORDING TIPS
--------------
- Prepare the session beforehand: log in (board@senus.com), data loaded, insights already generated (cached).
- Rehearse the chat question to avoid dead time; cut the wait in editing.
- Browser zoom at 110-125% so text is readable.
- Show Documents first (the workflow), then Financials (the analysis), and use Company as a brief example.
- Never show the .env file or API keys on screen.

FULL VIDEO SCRIPT
-----------------
(Approx. four to five minutes at a natural speaking pace. Text in square brackets is a
visual cue and is not spoken.)

[Start on the application’s Documents page.]

I built this application to solve a practical problem: important financial
information is locked inside long PDF reports, and turning it into something
people can review and compare takes time. The goal is not just to display a
dashboard. It is to make the journey from source document to decision-ready
information visible and reviewable.

[Show the navigation, then the Documents page.]

The application has three main areas. Documents is where an administrator
uploads a report and reviews its processing. Financials turns the confirmed
figures into KPIs and comparisons. Company brings together the report’s
narrative, such as customers, milestones and management. A viewer can explore
the published information, while uploading, correcting and publishing are
reserved for an administrator.

[Demonstrate selecting a PDF and document type, then show the detected periods.]

After upload, the system reads the PDF and looks for financial statements,
reporting dates and periods. Before extraction can continue, the administrator
confirms the period dates. This matters because a comparison is only meaningful
when the reporting periods have been identified correctly. If the headings or
dates do not agree, the workflow asks for review instead of quietly guessing.

[Show a flagged item, its source details, and the confirm or correct controls.]

For the figures, I use a deterministic parser and, when configured, a second
AI-assisted extractor. The application compares their results and retains
source details, including page references. When the extractors disagree, that
difference is flagged for review: it is not silently resolved by the AI.
An administrator can inspect the item, confirm it, or enter a correction.
Confidence factors also help bring less reliable items to the top of the review
list.

The system then runs accounting checks, such as whether revenue less cost of
sales matches gross profit, and whether the cash movement reconciles. If a
required value is missing, the check is not treated as proof that the numbers
are correct. The idea is to expose the evidence and the exceptions so a person
can decide what to publish.

[Move to Financials and select a KPI, then show the comparison controls.]

In Financials, reported values are distinguished from figures calculated by
the application. A derived KPI includes its formula, and unavailable data is
shown as unavailable instead of being replaced with zero or an invented
estimate. The dashboard also brings together the profit and loss, balance
sheet and cash flow, with charts to make trends and cost structure easier to
read.

For comparisons, I wanted the time direction to stay consistent. You choose
another period, and the application orders the pair chronologically, so the
earlier period is always the baseline. It shows changes in key metrics and a
waterfall that explains how reported line items contribute to the movement in
operating result. When the periods have different lengths, the interface warns
that the comparison is not like-for-like.

[Briefly show Company, an insight disclaimer, and the AI chat.]

Company is a good example of how narrative can sit alongside financial
evidence. Here, Senus is the sample report, but the important part is the
workflow: the narrative is extracted from the source, while AI insights and
chat are clearly labelled as interpretations rather than quotations. The
financial dashboard does not depend on AI-generated answers.

[Show the test folders or a final application screen.]

Behind the interface, the frontend uses React and TypeScript, and the API uses
FastAPI with a PostgreSQL database. The services run together with Docker
Compose. I added backend and frontend tests, and treated validation,
reconciliation, authentication and role-based access as part of the product,
not as afterthoughts.

The key design choice throughout was to make uncertainty visible: keep the
source, show what is calculated, flag disagreements, and leave missing
information missing. That is what turns a PDF into a report people can
actually inspect and use.
